# 우리 동네 빵집의 하루 — Treatment

Style: Watercolor Brush (`watercolor`). 30 s, 1920×1080, 60 fps. No voice; music, foley and hand-written Korean notes only (no subtitles, so no `.srt`).

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Journal kept over one day** — five pages (05:00, 07:30, 12:00, 16:00, 21:00), each a new sheet sliding over the last. Clear, but five page turns in 30 s is choppy and each page gets only ~6 s to paint in.
2. **One locked storefront, time changing over it** — a single painted façade; the sky, the light in the window and the bread on the shelf change from pre-dawn to night. The palette itself is the clock. Calm, readable in 30 s, one hero subject.
3. **A recipe on a tall sheet** — flour → dough → oven → shelf → a customer's hand, tilting down one tall page. About bread, not about the bakery's *day*.

**Chosen: 2**, opened and closed with a push/pull on one loaf. The topic is "a day", and the cheapest, most watercolour way to show a day is to let the washes behind one place change colour (palette as data). It also keeps the camera mostly locked, far from the demo's long lateral walk.

## 2. Logline and arc

A small neighbourhood bakery is painted into being before dawn, fills with bread and people through the day, sells out, and at night only one loaf — and a note for tomorrow — is left.

- Setup (0–10 s): a warm blob of pigment blooms on paper; the camera pulls back and it becomes a loaf glowing behind an oven window at 05:00; the shop paints itself in around it.
- Turn (10–22.5 s): the door bell rings, the shelves burst full, the day runs fast, loaves vanish beat by beat.
- Ending (22.5–30 s): night, music lifts, the camera leans in on the one remaining loaf while everything else fades back to paper; a hand note: "내일 새벽 다섯 시에 또".

## 3. Benchmark

- Picture-book city sketchbooks and "one window through the seasons" time-lapse shorts. **Learn**: one fixed viewpoint whose light changes tells time without words; small human silhouettes give scale. **Don't take**: any specific shop, character, layout or drawing.
- Slow-living cooking shorts (food close-ups with natural sound). **Learn**: the sound of the place (bell, oven, paper bag) carries the warmth; music stays small. **Don't take**: shots or music.

Camera moves used: pull-back, tilt, locked, push in, pull out, push-in with fade (5+ moves, one cut-free sheet).

**Signature shot**: shot 2, the pigment bloom that turns out to be the bread in the oven, with the shop painting itself in around it during a single pull-back.

## 6. Cue map

- Tempo 96 BPM, 4/4, 12 bars. Key: F major, warm and small.
- Bars 1–3: soft pad + celesta-like kalimba arpeggio, sparse (pre-dawn).
- Bar 4: music out (silence 1), oven hum + paper only.
- Bar 5 (10.0): door bell, then kalimba melody + glockenspiel; bass enters.
- Bar 6 (12.5): burst lands on beat 1 with a glockenspiel chord.
- Bars 7–9: fuller (plucks on eighths), one loaf vanishes per beat in bar 8.5–9.8, each with a pluck.
- Bar 10 (22.5): pad only, slows (ritardando feeling via longer notes).
- Bar 10.8 (24.4): silence 2, crickets.
- Bar 11 (25.0): single kalimba note → final chord at 26.25, rings to 30.

## 8. Text design

Korean only (the film's one language), so the style's Chinese-over-English stack becomes a single Korean line in the upper line's place and size. Serif: Noto Serif KR for the title. Hand notes: Gaegu (an OFL Korean handwriting face) in place of Caveat. Mono labels: IBM Plex Mono for times and temperatures (digits only). All fonts self-hosted, subset to the characters used.
